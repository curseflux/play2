# Self-grading rubric

My reconstruction of what's being scored, from the blurb's own language. Score
each 0–3. Be harsh; the point is to find the two weakest rows before the real test,
not to feel good.

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Found the decision** | Never states what is being decided | Mentions it | States decision, owner, deadline | Every section traceable to the decision |
| 2 | **Provenance discipline** | Compares numbers as given | Notes one difference | Tabulates version/grader/n | Uses a mismatch to reframe the question |
| 3 | **Arithmetic** | None | Quotes numbers | Recomputes one key quantity | Refusal-adjusts, uses a control arm as a ruler, sizes the noise |
| 4 | **Hypothesis space** | One hypothesis | Several, one bucket | Both measurement and substantive | Both buckets, ranked, credences, split where compound |
| 5 | **Direction test** | Absent | Implicit | Used on one candidate | Used to explicitly kill a candidate |
| 6 | **Discriminating tests** | "Investigate further" | Tests listed | Each hypothesis has a test | Cheapest decisive test per pair, with predicted outcomes |
| 7 | **Prioritisation** | Flat list | Loosely ordered | Ordered with costs | Costed in the scenario's units, totals fit, VOI-ordered, free tests first |
| 8 | **Deliberate omissions** | None | One line | Several, with reasons | Covers can't-change-decision / can't-finish / hold-in-reserve / not-mine |
| 9 | **Recommendation** | None or pure hedge | A leaning | A clear call | Call + conditional branch + named reversal trigger |
| 10 | **Error asymmetry** | Absent | Mentioned | Used | Recommendation shown robust to own uncertainty |
| 11 | **Calibration** | Bare assertions | Some hedging words | Consistent ladder | Ladder + numbers on load-bearing claims + no false precision |
| 12 | **Support labelling** | Absent | Occasional | Most key claims | Observation vs inference vs prior distinguished in-sentence |
| 13 | **Assumptions surfaced** | Premises smuggled | A few listed | Listed and relevant | Includes an assumption that threatens the author's own best claim |
| 14 | **What would change my mind** | Absent or generic | Listed | Concrete and directional | Says which conclusions each result would *not* move |
| 15 | **Selection effects** | Treats anecdotes as rates | Notes small n | Notes how evidence was found | Separates existence from frequency and plans the base-rate test |
| 16 | **Distractor handling** | Chases it | Ignores silently | Notes and defers | Defers with owner, and names the different decision it matters for |
| 17 | **Template completeness** | Sections empty | Thin sections | All filled | All filled with substance, depth matched to importance |
| 18 | **Writing** | Unstructured | Readable | Summary-first, signposted | Load-bearing considerations flagged; no padding; conclusion in 60s |
| 19 | **Integrity / judgement** | Misses or moralises | Notes it | Addresses the object level | Answers fairly, names limits of own authority, escalates in writing |

**Interpreting your score.** Rows 3, 7, 9, 10, 14 are where most otherwise-strong
answers lose ground, because they require doing something rather than writing
something. If you're below 2 on any of those, drill that row specifically.

**The five-minute version**, for checking a draft in the real test:

- [ ] Is there a number I should have divided by something?
- [ ] Does every hypothesis have a test, and does every test have a cost?
- [ ] Do the costs add up to less than the stated budget?
- [ ] Is there an actual recommendation, with a trigger that would reverse it?
- [ ] Does every substantive claim have a confidence attached?
- [ ] Have I said what I'm *not* doing?
- [ ] Is there one assumption listed that would hurt my own best argument?
- [ ] Are all template sections non-empty?
