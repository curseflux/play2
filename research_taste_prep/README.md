# Research taste practice pack

Start with [HOW_TO_PRACTISE.md](HOW_TO_PRACTISE.md), then open one case and the [response template](RESPONSE_TEMPLATE.md). Leave `answers/` closed until your timer ends.

## What this pack is based on

The supplied [assessment brief](../research_taste_assessment.txt) describes a **100-minute written assessment** about an ambiguous fictional situation during pre-release testing of a frontier model. It suggests about 20 minutes reading and 80 minutes writing. It emphasizes forming hypotheses from scattered evidence, prioritizing under real tradeoffs, and explaining assumptions and uncertainty. It says several interpretations can be reasonable and that a complete answer should come before refinements.

The actual assessment prohibits AI assistance. For realistic rehearsal, attempt these cases independently; use the answer keys afterward. No outside research or coding is needed for this pack.

The recommended background article, Luke Muehlhauser's [Reasoning Transparency](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/reasoning-transparency/) (December 1, 2017), was accessible online when this pack was created. Its central advice is to make conclusions easy to find, identify the considerations carrying the most weight, and disclose confidence and the kind of support behind important claims. It also encourages making the process and supporting material inspectable. Those are useful writing habits, not a demand for elaborate calculations or a transcript of every thought.

The local [OCR PDF](../reasoning_transparency%20OCR.pdf) is an alternative copy; the online article was used, so PDF extraction was unnecessary.

**All cases, organizations, figures, research budgets, model answers, and scoring criteria below are original practice material.** They are not leaked questions, a reconstruction of the real test, or an official rubric. The recommended word counts are practice constraints, not known assessment requirements. The article supports the brief writing advice above; it does not establish the fictional scientific claims in the exercises.

## Suggested use

If you have one session, do case 01 and spend 20 minutes reviewing it. If you have three sessions, do 01, 03, and 04. For a fuller preparation sequence, work through the cases in order, with a short drill between sessions. Choose case 05 now if you want practice distinguishing an information-processing explanation from an unintended-objective explanation.

Use the [self-review rubric](SELF_REVIEW.md) **before** reading a strong answer. Disagreeing with the answer key is acceptable. Check whether your alternative accounts for the evidence, fits the budget, identifies what would change your mind, and makes a usable decision.

The worked responses model a strong timed answer rather than an exhaustive research report. Their debriefs explain what earns credit in this practice pack, identify tempting mistakes, and describe defensible alternatives. Avoid memorizing their conclusions: the cases intentionally call for different decisions.
