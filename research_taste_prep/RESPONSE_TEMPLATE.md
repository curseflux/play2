## 1. Recommendation now

State the decision, its scope, the two most important reasons, and your confidence. Distinguish immediate containment from a final release judgment. Aim for 100-150 words.

## 2. Evidence and competing explanations

Identify three or four explanations. For each, give supporting evidence IDs, counterevidence or limitations, and a distinguishing prediction. Rank them without implying they must be mutually exclusive.

Name the observation carrying the most weight and the observation you are most worried about overinterpreting.

## 3. Prioritized investigations

For each selected investigation:

- Question and why it affects the decision.
- Comparison or intervention, controls, and outcome measure.
- Cost, order, owner or role, and dependencies.
- What different plausible results would imply and what you would do next.

Show the budget total. State one attractive investigation you are declining and why. If you invent a test, provide its cost and assumptions.

## 4. Decision rules and remaining uncertainty

What permits proceeding, keeps restrictions in place, or triggers stronger restrictions? Define the deployment scope and who needs to verify any condition. Do not claim an unrun test passed.

What are your two most consequential assumptions? What evidence would change your leading explanation? What would change the action even if the explanation stayed the same?

## 5. Strongest objection

Steelman a colleague's best objection. Say whether it changes your recommendation, your confidence, or the proposed scope.

## 6. Message to the decision-maker

Write a standalone message of at most 120 words: recommendation, important evidence, unresolved uncertainty, next checkpoint. Avoid unsupported claims about internal motives.
