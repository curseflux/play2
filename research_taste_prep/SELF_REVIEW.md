# Self-review rubric

This is an original practice rubric, not the assessment provider's scoring system. Use it to compare your own attempts, not to forecast your official score.

Score each dimension 0-3. A total out of 24 is a tracking device; the comments matter more.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decision | Missing | Vague preference | Concrete scope and action | Concrete action with conditions, checkpoint, and tradeoff |
| Evidence use | Invents or misreads facts | Mostly repeats packet | Separates evidence from inference; cites IDs | Identifies decisive evidence, weak sources, and dependence between observations |
| Hypotheses | One story asserted | Alternatives listed | Alternatives compared and ranked | Predictions discriminate explanations; overlap and counterevidence handled |
| Prioritization | Unbounded wish list | Ranking without rationale | Feasible top choices | Costs, opportunity costs, dependencies, and branching plan are explicit |
| Investigation design | No testable plan | Generic test | Controls and outcomes specified | Outcomes change beliefs and decisions; sampling and measurement problems addressed |
| Uncertainty | False certainty or no judgment | Generic caveats | Claim-specific confidence and assumptions | Specific reversal conditions; conclusions remain usable under uncertainty |
| Tradeoffs | Ignores costs or harms | Mentions both sides | Compares realistic alternatives | Explains why chosen scope is proportionate and engages best objection |
| Communication | Incomplete or contradictory | Complete but hard to use | Clear, complete, reasonably concise | A busy reader can find the decision and audit its basis quickly |

## Check before reading the answer

- Did I answer every required part, including the short message?
- Did I confuse a percentage-point change with a percentage change?
- Did I use the right denominator? Are repeated trials or selected examples being treated as independent samples?
- Did I treat an automated score, a source's interpretation, or a model's explanation as established ground truth?
- Did I propose a comparison that changes several factors at once without acknowledging that limitation?
- Do my chosen tests fit **all** budgets, including time, people, episodes, and dependencies?
- Did I explain what a null result would and would not show?
- Is my recommendation conditional on controls actually existing and being tested?
- Did I give a concrete route to relaxing restrictions, or explain why none is justified within this deadline?
- Did I distinguish a change of belief from a change of action?

## After reading the answer

Do not award yourself points for matching its conclusion. A different recommendation can be stronger if it handles the constraints better. Conversely, choosing the key's recommendation without the reasoning does not demonstrate the skill.

Write:

> My most important omission was ...
>
> The sentence where I sounded more certain than my evidence allowed was ...
>
> My highest-value improvement next time is ...

If a practice answer seems inconsistent with the case, identify the exact evidence IDs and budget lines. Challenging an answer key with evidence is good practice.
